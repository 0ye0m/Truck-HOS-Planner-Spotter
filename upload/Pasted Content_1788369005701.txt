2. SOURCE OF TRUTH
==================================================

Use the supplied FMCSA document:

"Interstate Truck Driver's Guide to Hours of Service for Property Carriers"

Use the supplied blank Driver's Daily Log image as the visual template/reference for generated logs.

The implementation should follow the supplied material, especially:

- 14-hour driving window
- 11-hour maximum driving limit
- 10 consecutive hours off-duty before a normal new driving period
- 30-minute consecutive break after 8 cumulative hours of driving
- 70-hour / 8-day rolling on-duty limit
- 34-hour restart
- on-duty vs driving vs sleeper berth vs off-duty distinctions
- required remarks/location information
- daily 24-hour graph grid

The FMCSA guide states that the 14-hour window starts when the driver starts work and that the driver can drive up to 11 hours during that window. Once the 14-hour period ends, driving cannot resume until the required qualifying off-duty period. 

The guide also states that the 30-minute break is required after 8 cumulative driving hours and can be satisfied by a consecutive non-driving period, including on-duty-not-driving, off-duty, or sleeper berth.

The 70/8 limit is a rolling 8-day on-duty limit and not simply a calendar week.

Do not treat these as generic "break every X hours" rules.
Implement the actual state-based rules.

8. DO NOT CONFUSE THESE THREE LIMITS
==================================================

The engine must independently track:

A. Driving hours
B. 14-hour duty window
C. 70-hour/8-day on-duty cycle

Example:

Driver can have:

10 driving hours
13 total window hours
but only 5 cycle hours remaining

The engine must recognize that the cycle limit can stop driving even when daily driving/window limits have remaining capacity.

Similarly:

Driver can be on duty after the 14-hour window, but cannot drive after the window expires.

Do not automatically classify every post-window activity as a violation.
Only driving after the window is prohibited.

10. DEFAULT SCHEDULING STRATEGY
==================================================

Implement a deterministic strategy.

At the beginning of a new driving period:

- Driver has up to 11 driving hours
- Driver has a 14-hour window
- Driver must take the required 30-minute non-driving break after 8 cumulative driving hours
- Driver must stop driving before the 14-hour window expires
- Driver must stop driving before the 11-hour driving limit
- Driver must stop driving when the remaining 70/8 cycle hours are exhausted

For long-haul planning, after reaching the legal driving capacity:

- schedule a 10-hour off-duty/sleeper period
- reset the daily 11-hour driving availability and 14-hour window

Prefer sleeper berth for overnight rest in generated logs because the supplied assessment specifically expects ELD-style daily logs and the FMCSA guide includes sleeper berth as a duty status.

==================================================
11. 30-MINUTE BREAK LOGIC
==================================================

This MUST be implemented correctly.

Track cumulative driving time since the end of the last qualifying 30-minute break.

When cumulative driving reaches 8 hours:

- driving cannot continue until a qualifying consecutive 30-minute non-driving period occurs

The break may be represented as:

OFF_DUTY
SLEEPER_BERTH
ON_DUTY_NOT_DRIVING

Do not split the 30 minutes into separate non-consecutive pieces.

Reset the driving-break counter after the qualifying 30-minute interruption.

Example:

Drive 4h
Drive 4h
=> 8h cumulative driving

Must insert 30-minute break.

Then:

Drive remaining available time.

==================================================
12. PICKUP AND DROPOFF
==================================================

Pickup duration = exactly 1 hour.

Dropoff duration = exactly 1 hour.

These are work activities, therefore represent them as:

ON_DUTY_NOT_DRIVING

They consume:

- 14-hour window
- 70/8 cycle

They do NOT consume driving hours.

The scheduler must consider whether pickup/dropoff causes the driver to exceed the 14-hour window or 70/8 cycle.
